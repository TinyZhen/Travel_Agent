## Slide 2. Alex has $600 for four days in Chicago and types one sentence
_about 34 seconds_

Meet Alex, a student we made up. Six hundred dollars. Four days. Chicago. Alex types one sentence.

> Point at the five boxes.

Flights, a hotel, sights, food and transit all have to add up, after every change.

A chatbot can do that sum and say it fits. **Nobody checks it.**

In one survey, eighty nine percent wanted AI help. Only twelve percent would let it decide alone.

## Slide 3. Roameo rests on three simple ideas
_about 26 seconds_

So we built Roameo on three ideas.

One. **AI agents do the legwork.** An agent is an AI helper with one job.

Two. **Code checks the money.** Plain code, not the AI, adds up every dollar.

Three. **You make the call.** Nothing is final until you say OK.

## Slide 4. AI agents ask first, then choose what to search
_about 41 seconds_

Idea one. First, the agents ask for what is missing. Alex never said where from, or when. So Roameo asked. A guessed city or date gives a useless plan.

Second, they choose the searches. For Alex, they compared eight flights, eighteen hotels and fifty nine events. Past weather said cold and rainy, so Roameo also looked indoors.

Third, plain words become changes. Say make it cheaper, and Roameo edits the plan instead of starting over.

## Slide 5. Code adds up every price and tries five fixes, from $1,235 to $780
_about 38 seconds_

Idea two. **Code checks the money.**

Alex's first plan came to twelve hundred thirty five dollars. More than twice the budget.

So code tries one fix at a time, up to five. A cheaper hotel. The cheapest flight. Free sights. After each fix, it adds everything up again.

Why code and not the AI? Code gives the same sum every time, and we can test it. An AI might not.

## Slide 6. Still $180 over, Roameo stops and asks Alex what to do
_about 40 seconds_

After five fixes, seven hundred eighty dollars. Still **one hundred eighty over**.

> Pause. Point at the orange box.

This is the moment that matters. Roameo does not hide the gap, or decide for Alex. It stops and asks. Raise the budget? Two nights? Or drive?

Idea three. **You make the call.**

In our tests, a higher budget makes the plan fit. Alex says OK and gets a calendar file. Roameo never books or pays. That choice stays with you.

## Slide 7. All 23 tests of what Roameo should do pass, with and without the AI
_about 49 seconds_

Does it work?

A test is a short chat we wrote in advance. We type it into Roameo, and code checks the answer. Did it ask for a missing date? It passes only if every check is right.

We ran the tests in demo mode, with sample prices. Then three times with the AI, since its answers can change.

All twenty three passed, every time. They also check that Roameo adapts, like indoor stops on cold days.

To be fair, we wrote these tests, and have no user study yet.

## Slide 8. Roameo never books or pays, and says what it cannot do yet
_about 32 seconds_

Responsible use follows the same three ideas. The AI asks first. Code shows its math. You decide.

There are no accounts. Your words go to the AI model only in AI mode. The searches get the city and dates, never your budget.

What can go wrong? The summary and tips come from the AI, and can be wrong.

## Slide 9. Other apps plan or sell trips, Roameo treats your budget as a limit
_about 36 seconds_

Who else helps? ChatGPT can write a trip plan. Expedia's Romie helps you plan and book. Apps like Mindtrip and Layla focus on ideas and day plans. We have not tested them.

Roameo sits in between. Not a booking site, not a chat window. It treats your budget as a **limit, not a wish**.

It is for students, budget travelers and small groups. Student clubs first.

## Slide 10. Anyone can try Roameo free, and a club can share one link
_about 42 seconds_

This is for students and the community. Demo mode is free, starts on a laptop in about three minutes, and needs no sign ups.

A club can share one link. A group of four sees two rooms and each person's share before anyone pays.

A class can read the code to see an AI that asks before it spends.
